# Pilot episode: The hidden language of 3D

**Target duration:** 25–35 seconds  
**Format:** 16:9 landscape  
**Topic:** Vector addition  
**Status:** first visual prototype; narration and rendered output not yet validated

## Voiceover draft

"What if one simple idea could describe a push, a flight path, or the motion of a planet? Meet the vector. A vector carries two things: how much, and in which direction. When two vectors act together, we can add their components and predict the result. This is one of the hidden languages connecting mathematics to the physical world. And once you see it, motion starts to look different."

## Scene timing
1. **0–3 s — Hook:** title appears over a deep navy background; subtle grid builds in.
2. **3–8 s — Define:** cyan vector appears with label a; amber vector b follows.
3. **8–15 s — Reveal:** dashed parallelogram construction shows the sum in lime.
4. **15–24 s — Understand:** component notation appears; narrator explains magnitude and direction.
5. **24–30 s — Payoff:** “Add the components. Predict the motion.” Hold for readability.

## Fact-check notes
- Vectors are represented by magnitude and direction (or components in a chosen coordinate system).
- The vector-sum construction is valid in the shown 2D coordinate plane.
- The animation is a visual introduction, not a complete treatment of vectors in three-dimensional space.
